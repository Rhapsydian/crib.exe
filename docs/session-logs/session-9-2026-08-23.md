# Session 9 — 2026-08-23

`/decision-session`, resolving the question banked since session 3:
exactly how a lost Control/Breach duel translates into Heat gained.

Opened by confirming a foundational reading before any formula could
make sense: losing a duel means losing that individual combat, not
instant run-death — Heat is the actual accumulating run-ending resource,
mirroring StS HP hitting 0. Confirmed. Landed the formula in one round:
a base Heat cost by encounter tier, adjusted by margin of loss — with
margin redefined for a push/pull meter that stops the instant it hits an
end (so "margin" can't mean overshoot): how far the player pushed
Control/Breach toward their *own* win before the enemy dragged it back,
not how badly they lost. Confirmed boss/core-tier losses are run-ending
outright, bypassing Heat entirely.

The user then raised a genuinely new thread: what happens to the
*network map* when you lose? Landed on: losing a regular/elite fight
ejects the player and **permanently** closes that node, forcing a route
around it — which implies a second, distinct loss condition (no route
forward remains). Confirmed scope: **every** layer's gatekeeper/boss loss
is run-ending, not just the final one — for two reasons, raising the
stakes deliberately, and a nice structural insight from the user that a
gatekeeper is by definition the only passage to the next layer, so
losing one is already an unavoidable dead end; making it instant
permadeath is just being direct about that rather than limping through
an already-lost run. Confirmed node closure must be permanent, or the
new loss condition could never actually trigger. Banked (explicitly
"idea space to explore," not designed): a future ability/passive that
lets a player bypass a closed node.

This gives the run **three distinct ways to end**: Heat maxing out,
a gatekeeper/boss loss in any layer, or running out of routes forward.
