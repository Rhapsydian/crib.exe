## Decisions resolved live (`docs/recalibration-design.md`)

- **R1** — prices are **Credit-equivalents measured in mirror duels**,
  not against skeleton accumulated state (which would be circular: step 3
  replaces the content the prices were measured against). Per-layer
  difficulty tables move to after step 3.
- **R2** — one **host-plus-difference** method for every verb; Charge
  priced through betas.
- **R3** — betas priced by **formula, validated by measurement**.
- **R4** — session 50's burstiness check folds into R3's validation.
- **R5** — the run economy (session 50's D1–D7) is **built in step 2**,
  and Trace is priced against a layer's Heat allowance.
- **R6** — fight Trace = **posted baseline + performance term** on the
  enemy's final Containment (the user's session-51 idea, reconciled with
  D2's transparency rule: the final value is on screen, unlike the hidden
  peak that sank the margin-of-loss scaler).
- **R7** — a loss costs **m × the node's worst-case win** — the user's
  call that a loss must cost more than any win, made structural.
- **R8** — the gatekeeper toll is a **gamble charged after the fight**,
  with a new ending, **"breached, then caught"**. The user's framing
  replaced my first recommendation (a fixed reserve, which created
  arithmetic, not a choice), and corrected my claim that a performance
  term there would be "hidden". Banked: meta-progression credits breaches
  — the user's "rep for how deep you got into the network".

## Findings worth keeping

- **The scripted player, not the economy, caused the first Heat
  collapse.** Victory fell from 42–82% to 0–20% with "breached, then
  caught" ending a third to half of runs — because the route-planner
  walked into every gatekeeper with no reserve for a toll it didn't know
  existed. Teaching it to budget the *posted* prices fixed it (victory
  18–69%, heat endings 5–28%). Fight Trace is now ~60% of all Heat, up
  from ~25%: the budget is about which fights to take, not walking.
- **`playCombat` is not seat-symmetric**: side 0 wins **65.5%** of a pure
  mirror (defender's deadline win, side 0 deals first). Every pricing
  duel is played in both seats; identical loadouts then tie *exactly*.
- **R2's planned instrument check was a tautology** — "Credit c prices
  at c" compares identical loadouts at Δ = c. Replaced by a wiring check,
  an off-grid resolution check, and a cross-verb validity check.
- **Prices are a staircase.** A Credit race is decided by turns-to-win;
  values needing the same number tie exactly on every seed. At a host of
  4 one step is ~1.2 Credit wide — a property of the game, not the
  instrument. Precise prices come from small hosts; authoring should
  think in turns-to-win, not decimals.
- **Prices add up**: Suppress and Ward priced together equal the sum of
  their parts within 0.02 at every host — the validity check.
- **The price table**: points verbs ~1:1 with Credit (Suppress/Ward ~0.95
  per point, Empower ~1 per boosted piece); Tempo and Disrupt scale with
  the loadout they act on (haste ¼ turn ≈ ⅓ of own output; the 15-point
  cooldown halves it; slow and forced discard ≈ 0.27 × opponent's
  output); forced discard halves against a weaker opponent.
- **Both Honeypot watch items got numbers.** Credit taken from the
  opponent is the strongest verb per point (~1.85×) and already bypasses
  Ward. Credit spent from your own Ward is worth only the Ward that would
  otherwise be wasted — Honeypot's conversion pays only when its Ward
  outruns what the opponent can hit, which is evidence for the user's A2
  concern.
- **Reveal Crib was dead and is fixed.** Priced at exactly zero: it
  revealed the crib after discards, for pegging, and nothing read it. The
  user's intent was discard-time knowledge; rebuilt so the opponent
  discards first and the revealing side sees their two crib cards. Now
  0.10 / 0.18 / 0.14, on a par with Reveal Hand.
- **An interrupted session lost a run's notification, not the run.** The
  background pricing job's output file ended with its exit line and all
  three results; nothing needed re-running.
