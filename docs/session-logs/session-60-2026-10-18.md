## Decisions (user, live)

- **Content before the style guide:** a guide would mostly codify the
  existing look; content polish pays more now.
- **Text audit** (`docs/archive/text-audit.md`, R1-R11): "subroutine", never
  "piece"; Hooks say what gets bigger and count "since it last fired";
  Empower "the next subroutine's Process Credits, Suppresses or Wards 1
  more"; Charge is "one extra count" (edge cases in the glossary); Haste
  and Slow spell out the initiative ("¾ of a turn (9 initiative)", from
  the live threshold in a fight); Breach/Containment by name; a glossary
  in the detail panel.
- **Class Mods** (`docs/archive/class-mod-redesign.md`, K1-K8): every class Mod
  triggers on a fifteen or a pair, three each. Lateral Movement
  (Blackhat, was Exploit Chain): 1 initiative per pair. Eidolon: Daemons
  grow half a fire's growth per fifteen. Warden: Suppress 0.5 and Ward
  0.5 per pair. Phase Shift (Wraith, was Siphon): the first fifteen each
  hand you deal, Ward 0.5 and 1 initiative -- a per-hand limit instead of
  tiny fractions (the user: small fractions read badly).
- **No enemy carries a class Mod** (K6).
- **Elite Mods** (`docs/archive/enemy-review.md`, EM1-EM6): Reverse Engineering
  (Threat Intel Feed), Rollback (Incident Response), Power Siphon
  (Blackout Cell), Escalation (Intrusion Alarm), Evidence Chain (Threat
  Hunter), Resource Quota (Hypervisor). Principle (from rejecting Dead
  Air): enemy Mods mustn't ask for in-fight reactions the player can't
  make -- the levers are the loadout and the Cribbage.
- **Content pass, enemies first** (CP1-CP4): three enemy-only effects
  (Trace, Wipe, Strip); twelve enemy subroutines, three per archetype;
  seven regulars and two deep elites; names read as defenders (the user:
  you play the intruder) -- Zero-Day Broker became Threat Intel Feed.
- **Sandbox rejected** after a with-and-without test (0-1 points);
  Resource Quota kept as a Mod (-13 to -23 points with full loadouts).
- **Reverse Engineering given an uncapped Hook** (Credential Stuffing ->
  Ping; option B, the steadier of two).

## Problem-solving notes

- **Fifteens are twice as common as pairs**
  (5.3 vs 2.7 a hand): a pair-triggered Mod pays about double per
  trigger.
- **Changing enemy Mods shifts every class:** the first class run mixed
  "my Mod changed" with "the gatekeepers changed"; re-tune the
  gatekeepers first, then judge the classes.
- **Tempo is pricier than the budget assumed:** every extra turn fires
  the whole loadout.
- **Kit power has blind spots:** its reference has no Hooks or Ward, so
  it valued Sandbox, Wipe and Strip at zero; win rate saturates against
  regulars. Parity on Containment fill against real-shaped loadouts
  fixed both.
- **Stronger Heat enemies give more Heat:** fights last longer.
- **Deleted part of `node_modules`:** a `git worktree remove --force`
  followed a junction into the real `node_modules`. Fixed with `npm ci`
  (after stopping the dev server, which held a native file). Lesson:
  delete a link before removing anything that contains one.
