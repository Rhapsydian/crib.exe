# Session 29 — 2026-08-27

First entry in this project's new `session-logs/` convention (started this
session, at the user's request during close-out). Numbered to match the
"session N" narrative already used throughout `BACKLOG.md`/`DESIGN.md`
comments and commit messages, rather than restarting at 1.

## Goal

Fix the Breacher stalemate finding from session 28's checkpoint-E balance
sweep: Breacher sat at 10.0% victory in a fresh `playRun` sweep, a sharp
outlier against the other 5 classes' 23–41.5% band, traced to Legacy
Firewall beating Breacher's real starting kit 18/20 times entirely via
attrition (0 genuine threshold wins).

## Key decisions

- **Give Breacher's own kit a real closer, rather than nerf the enemy.**
  Chose to reuse the exact `mitigationBanked`-accumulator mechanism the
  Neutral Archetype's Circuit Breaker already proved out (session 28),
  over the alternative of turning down Legacy Firewall/Regular-tier
  Encryption suppression magnitudes — the kit-side fix is class-scoped and
  addresses the root cause (Breacher's suppression pair never advanced its
  own gauge), while an enemy-side nerf would ripple into every class's
  matchups against Regular-tier Encryption enemies for a problem that was
  really Breacher-specific.
- **Replace Steady Hand, not add a 4th piece.** Starting kits are fixed at
  3 pieces per session 12's design (1 per specialized archetype + 1
  Cantrip); Steady Hand (the flavor Cantrip, flat always-fire suppression)
  was the weakest identity tie of Breacher's 3 pieces and the natural slot
  to repurpose.
- **Stop after the diagnosed fix, don't chase the broader gap reactively.**
  Once the fix was verified against its diagnosing matchup but the
  full-run number barely moved, further sweeps (see below) revealed
  Breacher is broadly outgunned by the whole pure-Encryption enemy family,
  not just prone to stalling. Decided live to leave the newly-tuned Lock
  Fatigue as-is and bank the broader finding for the real per-class
  magnitude/balance pass, rather than expand scope mid-session off three
  data points.

## Notable problem-solving notes

- The sweep harness (`npm run sweep -- enemy --enemy=<id> --vs=<classId>`)
  made the tuning-miss catch fast and cheap — running the exact diagnosing
  matchup at 200 seeds after each constant change turned "does this
  actually fix it" into a same-session, numbers-based check rather than a
  guess, consistent with this project's sweep-driven tuning discipline
  throughout Phase 5.
- The gap between "fixes the diagnosed matchup" and "fixes the class" is
  itself the most useful output of this session: a narrowly-scoped content
  fix can be completely correct and verified, while the sweep that
  motivated it turns out to be measuring only a small slice of a bigger
  problem. Worth remembering before treating any single enemy-matchup
  sweep as representative of a class's overall health.
