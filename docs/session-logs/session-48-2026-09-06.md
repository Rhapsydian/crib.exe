## Goal

Take another hard look at balance, turn-economy tuning controls, and
specifically the default activation threshold of 8 — `BACKLOG.md`'s
"Turn economy has no tuning dial" item, flagged since session 47.

## What the measurement found before any decisions

Two throwaway diagnostics plus a re-run of `occurrence-frequency.ts`,
120–150 seeds, `playerSkill=0.85`, real skill-dial enemy AI.

1. **What 8 actually bought.** Real scoring is ~13.4 pts/hand (player)
   and ~12.4 (enemy). With overshoot carrying, 8 is **~1.6 turns per side
   per hand** and 3.2–4.6 subroutine fires per hand. Not "roughly one
   good hand" — a metronome.
2. **The threshold is a player-power dial, not a tempo dial.** Sweeping
   6→20 symmetrically craters the player everywhere. Asymmetric runs
   isolate why: enemy fire counts barely move with the *enemy's* own
   threshold, player fire counts track it almost linearly.
3. **`WIN_THRESHOLD` cannot lengthen fights.** 50→75→100 barely moves
   short fights and converts long ones into deadline attrition (breacher
   0% → 99% attrition). `ESCALATION_SHRINK_PER_HAND` is *derived* from
   `(50 − 10)/10`, and `HARD_RESOLUTION_HAND` caps everything.
4. **Fight length is bimodal**, 2.7–16.7 hands against `combat.ts`'s own
   claimed 10–25.
5. **Flat tempo effects are denominated in the threshold.** `EP_SMALL`
   (2) is 25% of a turn at threshold 8 — why four content changes moved
   the Quarantine Ward only 8.4pp → 5.7pp.

## Design decisions resolved live

1. **`GAUGE_THRESHOLD` 8 → 12.** Not for the reason originally proposed.
   The skill-to-tempo *ratio* is scale-invariant, so raising it does not
   make turns "more meaningful" — the real argument is that 8 against a
   floor of 4 left threshold-manipulation content **nowhere to live**:
   every reducing piece collapsed to "go to the floor." The user's own
   rationale: meta-progression should be able to buy the threshold down,
   and that space needs range. Accepted as a global nerf.
2. **Tempo effects become fractions of a turn/threshold**, not point
   counts, so nothing silently rescales when the base moves.
3. **A per-enemy tempo knob**, shipped as `gaugeThresholdScaler`.
4. **The reduction floor is a fraction of each side's own *starting*
   threshold**, not a global base — the only genuinely scale-free option.
   Accepted consequence: meta-progression compounds.
5. **Fight-length dispersion deferred** to its own session — it is
   content-shaped, and every fight-length number measured today is
   invalidated by this session's own change.
6. **`WIN_THRESHOLD` split** into `BREACH_THRESHOLD` and
   `CONTAINMENT_THRESHOLD` (raised mid-session by the user).
7. **Escalation derives per side, and meta-progression gets a socket.**

## Problem-solving notes

**Checkpoints A and B were verified behaviour-neutral empirically, not
argued.** Re-running the session's own measurement grid at the unchanged
threshold of 8 reproduced three of four pairings byte-identically. Ghost
drifted one seed in 120 — integer rounding now applied after the
Corrupted multiplier instead of leaving fractional gauge points behind.

**Checkpoint B introduced a real bug that 781 passing tests did not
catch.** `improvedPayloadMagnitude` adds a flat `+3` (Merge) or `+1.5`/
`+3` (the Primed passives) to a payload amount — against a *fraction*
that means "plus three whole turns." Merging `cold-call` would have
produced a 3.6-turn instant self-haste per fire, exactly the
non-terminating-fight shape session 47's `pointsCooldown` exists to
contain. No test merged a tempo piece. Fixed generically in `7c1d733`.

**Two of my own predictions were wrong, and the measurements said so.**
Firewall Prime, predicted most turn-responsive, moved least in fires
across a 2× threshold range — its output is reactive and tick-driven.
Adaptive Threat, predicted indifferent, responded most: its pieces are
rarely ready, but readiness is only *checked* on its own turns. The
three-regime model came from measuring the player side and was
over-extended to enemies.

**The Quarantine Ward prediction held exactly.** Its fires go *up* as it
is slowed (9.1 → 9.7), because slowing lengthens the fight and its
`total-quarantine` nudge rides DoT/HoT ticks. The tempo dial cannot fix
the case that motivated it — flagged when scoped, confirmed when built.

**The instrument audit found a test that had never tested its subject.**
`burners.test.ts`'s full-run smoke test was titled "with real activation
in every context" but asserted `combat + map > 0` — an OR the map path
satisfied alone. Combat activations were **zero**: combat-context Burner
activation had never been exercised end-to-end. The cause was in the
test, which declared a `combatStrategy` and then passed
`burnerActivationStrategies: undefined` with a comment claiming run.ts
threads its own — contradicting the option's own doc, since that option
was added at checkpoint J *specifically for this test*.

**The units bug had one more instance, and it was this session's own
casualty.** `choked` writes a `debuffMag` constant straight into an
initiative threshold. A common Choke was a 37.5% slow at base 8; moving
to 12 silently cut it to 25% — precisely the invisible rescale
checkpoints A and B existed to prevent, missed because the scope note
enumerated only `instantManipulation`'s targets.

## Balance outcome — worse, and knowingly so

150 seeds, `--acquisition=synergy --traversal=opportunistic`, skill 0.85:

| class | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Ghost | 32.7 | **42.0** |
| Saboteur | 30.0 | 23.3 |
| Operator | 23.3 | 18.0 |
| Blackhat | 21.3 | 14.7 |
| Breacher | 15.3 | 7.3 |
| Warden | 10.7 | 6.7 |

The nerf landed on five classes and **inverted on Ghost**. Dispersion
widened from 22pp to 35.3pp. The baseline's traversal was never
recorded, so the sweep was re-run on `beeline` to check comparability
(ghost 38.0 there — above baseline under both, so not an artefact).
At 150 seeds the SE is ~2.6–4pp, so the five-down/one-up *pattern* is
the robust finding, not any single number.

**Leading hypothesis, explicitly not a conclusion:** the tempo content is
ratio-neutral by construction, so the pieces do not explain Ghost.
Raising the threshold reduces *total actions for both sides*, and a
denial/outlast kit benefits from a world with fewer actions while an
offensive kit pays for them. If that holds, `GAUGE_THRESHOLD` is an
**archetype-preference dial**, not a difficulty one, and the rebalance
must not lean on it.

`gatekeeper-check.ts` also shows a non-monotonic curve: layer 1 71.7%,
layer 2 **55.1%**, layer 3 94.6%, layer 4 67.3%. Layer 2 is the hardest
layer in the game.
