## Goal

Session 38 left three open "next up" candidates. Asked broad-strokes
which to take; the user picked the still-banked middle-ground/node-type-

## Key decisions

Resolved live, one at a time, per this project's `.claude/dev-session.md`
override:

- **Mechanism**: my first draft scoped the strategy to fights-only
  detours (Shop/Event/Safehouse "for their own sake" out of scope). The
  user corrected this directly — Shops and Events are real acquisition
  opportunities, and Safehouse's Rest extends how much of a layer is
  even reachable, with Merge a direct power increase. Revised to treat
  all node types as real, if less direct, power sources.
- **Weighting**: fights always top priority (direct, reliable). Heat-high
  *or* material-high both pull toward Safehouse; Data-high pulls toward
  Shop; Event only pulls when Heat, material, *and* Data are all low
  together (the user's own addition — a low-resource fallback gamble,
  not a general-purpose detour).
- **Rest-vs-Merge tiebreak** at a Safehouse when both Heat and material
  triggers fire at once: **Heat wins** (the user's explicit call — it's
  the run-ending resource, safety-critical in a way Merge's power gain
  isn't).
- **Scope/process**: given the small size (one traversal strategy, one
  safehouse strategy, a signature widen, CLI wiring — not a
  Mods/Burners-Events-sized arc), the user asked to implement in the
  same sitting rather than stopping at a written spec the way sessions
  30-33/36-37 did for larger arcs.

| saboteur | 33.0% | 51.5% | 50.5% |

Blackhat is the clearest validation: explore's Heat fragility (session
38's own finding) tanks it to 20.0%; opportunistic's Heat-safety-reserve
protects it, landing at 26.5% (heat-maxed roughly halved vs. explore,

Elsewhere opportunistic tracks close to explore, ahead of beeline.

**Finding 1 — the noRoute anomaly, investigated and explained (not a
bug)**: opportunistic showed a *higher* `noRouteRemains` rate than
explore for breacher/operator/ghost despite being the more
Heat-conservative strategy. Instrumented both strategies on 300 shared
seeds: opportunistic visits *more* distinct fight nodes per run than
explore (e.g. breacher 9.05 vs 7.58) while using *fewer* total moves
(19.07 vs 23.33) — because explore burns moves on every Safehouse/Shop/
Event node indiscriminately (zero closure risk, but wasted budget) and
its node selection isn't actually nearest-first (node-array order, a
pre-existing quirk), while opportunistic always takes the true shortest
path and skips non-fight nodes unless a real pull condition fires. Every
sampled noRoute-ending run closed exactly 1 fight node before ending
(matches session 20's "resilience guarantee only promises safety against
one closure" finding) — so engaging more distinct fights per run is
more independent rolls against that single-point-of-failure risk. Net
victory *counts* stay essentially level (82 vs 82 breacher, 129 vs 123
operator, 113 vs 114 ghost) — the extra engagement converts into a mix
of extra wins and extra noRoute losses, not a clean win. Not a defect —
an honest reflection of the "leaning into either extreme is a real
tradeoff" framing already in `DESIGN.md`.

**Finding 2 — banked, not acted on: does `HEAT_PER_MOVE` need bumping?**
The user asked directly. Checked before answering: for 5 of 6 classes,
movement Heat is already the dominant Heat source, but runs still end at
32-42% of `HEAT_MAX` on average via quarantine/noRoute, `heatMaxed` at
0/300 — real headroom, Heat isn't the current bottleneck. **Blackhat is
the exception and the reason not to bump it casually**: 72.7/100 average
final Heat, 26% heat-maxed rate *at the current value*, driven by a
combination of its own kit's Heat taxes (session 38's fix) and movement
compounding on top — a global bump would land hardest on the class least
able to absorb it, risking undoing last session's fix. User's call:
leave `HEAT_PER_MOVE` as-is, bank the question for the actual balance
pass, where a candidate value can be tested against the full roster
(Blackhat especially) the same empirically-tested-candidates way session
38 tuned Blackhat/Breacher, rather than picked by feel now.

## Part 2 — the balance pass begins, then pivots into methodology and a real engine gap

The session didn't end at the close-out above — the user asked to start
the balance pass on the remaining classes right after, and it grew into
a much bigger arc than "fix a few numbers": a real methodology critique,
a new piece of permanent diagnostic infrastructure, and a genuine
difficulty-curve engine gap, fixed and empirically tuned.

### Null Session: found, chased, then mostly dissolved

Matchup-swept Saboteur/Operator/Warden/Ghost against all 12 gatekeepers
(100 seeds each, bare starting kits, `sweep.ts`'s `enemy` mode shape),
then widened to all 6 classes for the worst outliers. Two real findings,
distinguished from the already-accepted "Firewall Prime/Incident
Response are broadly hard, left alone" call (session 38): **Null
Session** was a sharp *bimodal* outlier — Operator 96% vs. 0-8% for
5 of 6 other classes — not a spread-the-pain gatekeeper. **Ghost** had a
real gap against Exploit/Malware-leaning gatekeepers (Adaptive Threat 0%,
Total Compromise 17%) despite dominating Root-heavy ones.

Traced Null Session's mechanism precisely via ablation (drop each kit
piece, re-sweep): **Zero Trust alone** was the entire problem — its
`instantCounterPush` amount (`CAPPED.rare` = 18) wipes 36% of the win
threshold in one reactive proc; Cron Job/Full System Compromise/Circuit
Breaker barely moved the needle (confirmed against Ghost Process, which
shares the same tempo-denial pair and is perfectly winnable). Tested a
bespoke, Null-Session-only tempered copy (amount 18 → 11 =
`UNCOMMON.burst`) that fixed the near-0% wall without touching the
shared pool piece (so Firewall Prime's own already-decided matchup stays
untouched) — **but never shipped it**, because the next step (a
realistic-state re-test) showed it probably wasn't needed at all.

### The methodology correction that mattered most

Asked directly how these matchup tests were actually run: bare
`CLASS_STARTING_LOADOUTS`, `playCombat`'s own dumb baseline AI
(`discardLowestTwo`/`playLowestLegal`) on *both* sides — not the real
skill-dial AI every actual gatekeeper encounter uses via
`strategiesForFight`. The user pushed back hard on testing worst-case
floors instead of real play: "isn't the balance sweep supposed to be
actually playing the game, all of its pieces?" Investigating that
question surfaced a bigger, previously-unstated fact: **every real fight
in this game, and every balance sweep since session 27, has the player
side playing Cribbage at the dumbest possible level** while enemies scale
up by tier/layer (`strategiesForFight`'s comment: "Only the enemy...
gets a skill-derived strategy"). Session 24 tested giving the player
real skill once, as a side investigation; it never became the default.

Built real infrastructure instead of another one-off script:

- **`onBeforeGatekeeperFight`** (`run.ts`): an additive, zero-effect
  observational hook, fired once right before each gatekeeper fight
  resolves, snapshotting the player's *real* accumulated `RunPlayerState`
  at that exact moment (whatever a real `playRun()` actually picked up —
  installed loadout, bench, Mods, Burners, Data) plus the
  `layerIndex`/`fightsResolved` a real `resolveFight` call would use.
  Verified with a dedicated test proving exact fire count/timing and
  that layer 1's capture is genuinely pre-acquisition (still exactly the
  3-piece starting kit).
- **`scripts/gatekeeper-check.ts`** (new, permanent): consumes that hook.
  Deliberately does *not* try to average a loadout across seeds (acquired
  content can't be meaningfully blended into one coherent object) —
  instead runs N real seeds per class (`opportunisticTraversal` + the
  usual legal-not-good defaults), captures every gatekeeper actually
  reached, and re-fights each captured *real* state in isolation against
  the real production skill-dial enemy AI at a chosen player skill
  (0.75 = matches the gatekeeper's own base skill, "fair"; 1.0 =
  "strong"), aggregating win rate per (class, gatekeeper, player skill)
  and, after a follow-up ask, per layer too.

**The 300-seed realistic re-sweep reversed the Null Session finding
almost entirely** — 93.5%/93.5%/64-71%/100%/65-69%/82-87% across the 6
classes, once real acquired power was in the picture. The near-total
lockout was mostly an artifact of testing bare day-one kits against a
worst-case floor. **The Zero Trust tempering fix was shelved, not
shipped** — it may not be needed now that real data exists instead of a
guess. Firewall Prime turned out to be the real remaining outlier
(Warden specifically: 4.5-5.7%, flat regardless of player skill) —
flagged, not yet acted on.

### The per-layer difficulty curve: a real, structural engine gap

Asked directly whether per-layer difficulty scaling had been
implemented. It hadn't, beyond a small skill-dial step
(`LAYER_SKILL_STEP` = 0.03) — `DESIGN.md`'s "meant to be very
challenging for the layer it's presented at" was pure authoring intent,
enforced by nothing. `gatekeeper-check.ts`'s new by-layer aggregation
(added specifically to check this) confirmed it empirically and
unambiguously, every class: layer 1 was the *hardest* tier (77.4%
aggregate) and it got progressively easier (L2 88.4%, L3 98.8%, L4
93.9%) — backwards from intent, because player power compounds across
layers faster than the tiny skill-dial step could ever offset.

Built a real fix, resolved live one decision at a time per this
project's `.claude/dev-session.md` override:

- **Magnitude scaling, not just skill** — a per-layer multiplier applied
  to enemy payload magnitudes at combat-resolution time (never baked
  into the static roster data). Multiplicative, not additive (a flat
  bonus would hit low- and high-magnitude payloads unevenly). Reused
  `merge.ts`'s existing magnitude-dispatch machinery
  (`improvedPayloadMagnitude`) rather than duplicating the payload-kind
  switch — refactored into a shared `transformPayloadMagnitude` core,
  with the new multiplicative `scaledPayloadMagnitude` as a sibling to
  the existing additive one.
- **Two different shapes, the user's own call**: regular/elite (which
  can legitimately appear at several layers) scale *live*, computed
  per-encounter from one shared tier-agnostic formula
  (`enemyMagnitudeScaler`, pinned to no scaling during the opener
  window). Gatekeepers (which never repeat across layers) instead get an
  **explicit, individually authored `magnitudeScaler` field** on
  `EnemyDefinition` — seeded uniformly per layer as a starting point, but
  a real standalone tuning knob per gatekeeper from here on (e.g.
  Firewall Prime, already the roster's hardest fight, is flagged as an
  obvious future candidate to dial back rather than up).
- **Confirmed wanted, explicitly deferred**: independent per-side
  initiative/win-gauge thresholds (asymmetric Breach/Containment
  difficulty), motivated by both this scaling work and future
  meta-progression. Currently `CombatOptions`/`createCombatState` share
  one threshold across both sides — real plumbing work, scoped as its
  own future pass rather than bundled in here.

**Tuned empirically, not guessed**: first candidate step (0.15/layer,
gatekeepers seeded 1.0/1.15/1.3/1.45) narrowed the layer-1-vs-4 gap but
didn't close it — L4 still an easier 85.6% win rate against L1's harder
77.4% (win rate is inverse to difficulty: lower = harder). Re-swept at
double the step (0.3/layer, 1.0/1.3/1.6/1.9): L4 dropped to 76.9%,
landing at parity with L1's 77.4% (within noise, given L4's smaller
surviving-run sample size) — the real target: a run's final stretch
should be at least as hard as its start. Landed here. Layer 3 remains a real, separate
outlier (94.1%, essentially unmoved by either step) — it was already the
easiest layer before any scaling, so it's very likely a genuine
kit-design weakness (its own trio: Total Compromise/Adaptive
Threat/Silent Corruption), not something a multiplier can fix — flagged
for a future content-level look, not acted on here.

Also, at the user's request: **removed `maxHands`/`FIGHT_MAX_HANDS`
outright** (`combat.ts`/`encounters.ts`/`scripts/sweep.ts` and every
test that referenced it) — `HARD_RESOLUTION_HAND` (session 27) has
unconditionally forced every real fight to resolve by hand 20 since it
shipped, so the old bound had been genuinely vestigial the whole time,
"left in place as a defensive outer bound" rather than removed at the
time. Two tests whose entire premise was forcing early exhaustion via an
artificially tiny `maxHands` had no way to construct that scenario
anymore — rewritten to assert the real current behavior instead of
removed outright. Found and fixed two stale comments along the way (a
specific hand-count claim and an escalation-start-hand claim, both
predating later retunes that were never reflected back into their own
comments).

  methodology question mattering more than the bug it was chasing: the
  original "0% for 5 of 6 classes" finding was real and reproducible,

  candidates discipline as every prior balance fix (Blackhat/Breacher,
  session 38) — pick a value, measure, adjust, re-measure — rather than
  guessing a "should be enough" number once and moving on.
